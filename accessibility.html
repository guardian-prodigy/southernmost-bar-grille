Accessibility

Island time should be open to everyone.

Southernmost is committed to providing an inclusive digital and in-person experience.

Updated July 24, 2026

1. Our commitment

Southernmost aims to make its website, menu, ordering, reservations and guest communications usable by people with diverse abilities, devices and assistive technologies. The goal is to follow recognized accessibility practices and continually improve based on testing and feedback.

2. Digital accessibility features

  • Semantic headings, landmarks and form labels.
  • Keyboard-accessible navigation, menu controls, drawers and dialogs.
  • Visible focus indicators and a skip-to-content link.
  • Text alternatives for meaningful images.
  • Responsive layouts that support zoom and small screens.
  • Reduced-motion support for guests who prefer less animation.
  • High-contrast text and controls designed to remain readable over imagery.

The page-turning menu is paired with a searchable menu grid so the same content is available without drag gestures or three-dimensional interaction.

3. Ordering alternatives

Guests are never required to use the digital table-ordering experience. A server can provide a physical menu, explain items, enter an order, review the tab and complete payment. Guests can request help at any point.

4. In-person access

Guests may use the reservation or event inquiry tools to share mobility, seating, communication, dietary or other accommodation needs in advance. Staff should make reasonable efforts to provide accessible seating and service consistent with venue conditions and applicable law.

5. Accessibility feedback

If any part of the experience creates a barrier, describe the page, feature, device, browser and assistance needed. Feedback helps prioritize improvements.

Request assistance
